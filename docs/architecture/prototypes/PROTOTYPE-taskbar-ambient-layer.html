Taskbar ambient numbers

The question: When Windows-Hinting keeps numbers on the taskbar for a Talon user to read aloud, and the taskbar shortcut then adopts those same numbers for selection, does the numbering model hold up? In particular: how are numbers handed out as apps open and close, what happens when the taskbar changes while you are mid-sentence, and can a spoken number ever open the wrong app?

The left side shows a simulated screen and every piece of state. Change the taskbar, let time pass, press keys, and watch the numbers. "Read the numbers" records what you saw, so a later selection can be checked against it. Nothing here touches the real taskbar.

Questions this demo is for

  1. How should new numbers be handed out? Reusing the lowest free number keeps numbers short, but a closed app's number can go to a new app seconds later. Never reusing is safe but numbers grow. Positional renumbering is what Talon does today.
  2. During an adopted session, freeze everything, or let numbers follow their buttons? The engine decision (#108 rule 14) says freeze everything. A reflow or display change then leaves numbers sitting over the wrong buttons until the session ends.
  3. Enter on a number whose button has gone: this demo beeps and keeps the session open. Should it end the session instead?
  4. Shortcut pressed while the display is still settling: the pending update is applied first (as decided), but the taskbar may not have finished laying out. Wait, or accept a brief misplacement?
  5. Silent changes (gate F): changing button labels or combining fires no event, so numbers drift until something else happens. Is a slow backstop check needed for that case only?
